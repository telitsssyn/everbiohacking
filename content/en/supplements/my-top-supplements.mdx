<TLDR>
Erinacine A (Erinamax by ND) and bacopa — taken together, for neurons. Creatine — every day. Curcumin with piperine — in cycles, against systemic inflammation. Everything else is vitamins and minerals, and only the ones where I have a confirmed deficiency: D3 with K2, B-complex, iron, iodine, selenium with vitamin E, magnesium as glycinate, calcium, zinc, potassium.
</TLDR>

Supplements will not replace sleep, food, and training. That is not a polite disclaimer but an order of priority: until the foundation is in place, any supplement returns less than it could. Once it is, a short and deliberate list gives you the few percent that decide things over a long horizon.

This is not a list of everything I have tried. This is what survived the cut.

<Callout title="The core principle">
I don't take vitamins and minerals "just in case". A supplement enters the stack when a test shows a deficiency, and leaves when that deficiency is closed. So the list below isn't a prescription to copy — it's a snapshot of my own bloodwork. Where to start testing: [Key blood tests](/en/protocols/tracking/basic-blood-tests).
</Callout>

## 1. Erinacine A extract

A natural active compound from the mycelium of the lion's mane mushroom that stimulates nerve cell growth. In practice that means neuroplasticity: new material lands better, skills form more easily. I take it paired with bacopa.

- **My choice:** Erinamax by ND.
- **Cycle length:** three months minimum.
- **Protocol:** 1000 mg before sleep (if your sleep gets worse, move it to a time that suits you).

## 2. Bacopa

Bacopa works slowly, through synaptic plasticity. It mostly acts on memory consolidation — on how firmly what you've already learned sticks.

I take erinacine and bacopa **together**: erinacine works on neuron growth and plasticity, bacopa on locking the result in.

- **My choice:** _brand to be confirmed_
- **Cycle length:** three months minimum.
- **Protocol:** _dose to be confirmed_

## 3. Creatine

The most researched supplement in the world, and mistakenly treated as purely a sports supplement. For me creatine is primarily about the head: it raises brain phosphocreatine stores, which means less mental fatigue on long tasks and better working memory. Muscle and strength come as a bonus.

Creatine monohydrate is much the same everywhere — it's hard to get the choice wrong.

- **Protocol:** 5 g (one scoop) in the morning, every day, no cycling.

## 4. Curcumin with piperine

The strongest natural anti-inflammatory I have tried. I run it in cycles to bring systemic inflammation down — across the body generally and in the joints specifically.

Piperine here is not a nice extra but a condition of the thing working. Curcumin on its own barely absorbs: it dissolves poorly and the liver clears it fast. Piperine (black pepper extract) blocks that clearance and raises curcumin bioavailability roughly twentyfold. Curcumin without piperine is money thrown away.

- **My choice:** NOW Foods, Turmeric Curcumin with BioPerine.
- **Protocol:** 1 capsule in the morning and 1 at lunch, in cycles.

## 5. Vitamins and minerals for deficiencies

Everything below I top up not "for general health", but because bloodwork showed a shortfall.

### D3 + K2

I don't take D3 without K2. Vitamin D increases calcium absorption, and K2 decides where that calcium goes: into bone or into arterial walls. Given that my stack also includes standalone calcium, this pairing is non-negotiable.

- **Protocol:** D3 — 2000 IU in the morning. K2 — _dose to be confirmed_

### B vitamins

Insurance on methylation and energy metabolism. I buy complexes in bioavailable forms — methylfolate rather than folic acid, methylcobalamin rather than cyanocobalamin.

- **My choice:** Life Extension, BioActive Complete B-Complex.
- **Protocol:** 1 capsule in the morning and 1 at lunch.

### Iron

The most temperamental item on the list. Iron is not taken blind: excess is as harmful as deficiency, so here I go by ferritin, not by how I feel.

- **Protocol:** _form and dose to be confirmed_
- **Important:** on an empty stomach, with vitamin C. Never alongside calcium, zinc, coffee, or tea — they cut absorption.

### Iodine

Raw material for thyroid hormones, which set your baseline metabolism and energy. Iodine-poor soil is near-universal, and subclinical hypothyroidism with brain fog grows out of exactly that.

- **My choice:** NOW Foods, Kelp.
- **Protocol:** 150 mcg in the morning.

### Selenium + vitamin E

I take these as a pair. The thyroid needs selenium to convert T4 into T3 — meaning without it, iodine only works at half strength. Selenium and vitamin E also back each other up as antioxidants, so pairing them is the logical move.

- **Protocol:** _doses to be confirmed_

### Magnesium (glycinate)

Glycinate for tolerability, and for the glycine itself: the form doesn't upset the gut and sits well with sleep.

- **My choice:** Doctor's Best, Magnesium Glycinate.
- **Protocol:** 200 mg an hour before sleep.

### Calcium

I only top this up because my diet falls short of it. Supplemental calcium makes sense only alongside D3 and K2 — otherwise there's a fair chance of routing it to the wrong place.

- **Protocol:** _form and dose to be confirmed_

### Zinc

Immunity, testosterone, skin, taste and smell. Zinc competes with iron and copper, so taking high doses long-term without monitoring is a bad idea.

- **Protocol:** _form and dose to be confirmed_

### Potassium

Almost always deficient in anyone eating few vegetables and a lot of prepared food, and almost always ignored. Works in concert with sodium and magnesium: blood pressure, cramps, exercise tolerance.

- **Protocol:** _form and dose to be confirmed_

## How I space the minerals out

Half the minerals on this list interfere with each other if you throw them down in one handful. My rules:

- **Iron — on its own.** Empty stomach, with vitamin C, at least two hours away from calcium, zinc, coffee, and tea.
- **Calcium — never with iron or zinc.** It suppresses absorption of both.
- **Magnesium — at night.** That also takes it out of competition with calcium and puts it to work on sleep.
- **Zinc — not on an empty stomach**, or it turns your stomach. And not in the same dose as iron.
- **Curcumin — never with iron.** Curcumin binds iron and cuts its absorption — a separate reason to keep iron away from everything else.
- **D3, K2, E and curcumin — with a meal containing fat.** All four absorb markedly worse without it.

<Callout title="Important">
What works on me may not work on you, or may harm you. This goes double for iron and zinc: those are not dosed by eye. Get tested before topping up anything on this list.
</Callout>
